Challenge 2027 
Introduction 
The MathWorks SAE Aero Design Simulation Challenge provides a platform for Aero Design teams 
to virtually test and demonstrate flight-readiness through flight simulation in a virtual 
environment. Teams download Virtual Environment for SAE Aero Design from File Exchange 
The project contains the 
• simulation model and supporting files required to configure the aircraft, 
• connect an RC controller,  
• virtual environment to fly missions,  
• record flight data and  
• prepare the submission.  
Teams customize the model parameters to represent their competition aircraft and may also 
modify the Aircraft Dynamics subsystem to represent the dynamics of their aircraft. This challenge 
recognizes teams that demonstrate innovative and effective use of MATLAB or Simulink to design 
their aircraft, develop a digital twin, and perform simulated flight missions in Simulink.

Award Prizes and Recognition 
The top three teams in the MathWorks SAE Aero Design Simulation Challenge will receive the 
following awards 
• First place: $500 
• Second place: $300 
• Third place: $200 
Winners will be announced at the SAE Aero Design awards ceremony and will receive certificates 
of achievement. They will also be invited to write a blog for the Student Lounge Blog  and 
collaborate on a social media post with the @matlab_students Instagram channel. 
Winners will be ranked in a single pool across the Regular, Micro, and Advanced classes; there will 
be no separate awards for each class. Judge's decision is final and binding. 
Challenge Criteria and Eligibility 
The challenge is open to teams participating in the SAE Aero Design 2027 competition in the 
Regular, Micro, and Advanced classes. Each team can make only one submission. 
Teams will be assessed on two components: 
• Flight missions in the virtual environment 
o Teams must download the Virtual Environment for SAE Aero Design from MATLAB 
File Exchange.  
▪ Teams must not modify the Environment subsystem, Calibrated Fixed-
Wing input subsystem, and related any supportive functions (Refer figure-
1). 
▪ Teams must change aircraft parameters with their aircraft data, may modify 
the internal  architecture of the Aircraft Dynamics subsystem and 
Visualization, while retaining the interface (Refer figure-1). 
o  Teams will fly one mission in each of the following environments: 
▪ Sea Level Standard 
▪ Either competition site as appropriate:  
• Aero Design East - Lakeland, FL  
• Aero Design West - Van Nuys, CA 
o Teams must submit the best recorded flight from each environment along with the 
complete MATLAB Project and an engineering report. 
o Both scored missions must be flown manually by a team pilot using an RC 
transmitter or joystick connected to the template. Autopilots and scripted control 
inputs are not allowed for the scored missions.

▪ Takeoff and climb (5 points) 
• Start from rest, leave the ground, and stay at or above 1.64 ft (0.5 
m) above ground level (AGL) for at least 0.5 s. 
• Complete the takeoff within your class takeoff limit. 
• Climb to the cruise altitude of 98.4 ft (30 m) and continue in the 
takeoff direction. 
• A takeoff within the class limit earns 5 points. A takeoff beyond 
the limit, or a flight with no detected takeoff, earns 0 takeoff 
points. 
▪ First turn (5 points) 
• Do not start the first turn before reaching your class first -turn 
distance from the origin. 
• Turn 180 degrees. Clockwise and counterclockwise circuits are 
both accepted. 
• Completing the first 180-degree turn earns 5 points. 
▪ Cruise 
• Fly the opposite-direction leg back past the origin. 
• Continue until you are at least the first -turn distance from the 
origin on the other side. 
▪ Second turn (5 points) 
• Turn 180 degrees in the same direction as the first turn. 
• Start this turn at least the first-turn distance from the origin, on 
the opposite side from the first turn. 
• Completing the second 180-degree turn earns 5 points, but only 
if the first turn was valid.

▪ Final approach (5 points) 
• Return on the runway heading to the departure sector. This 
closes a true 360-degree circuit. 
• Descend for landing. 
• Returning to the departure sector earns 5 points, but only if both 
turns were valid. 
▪ Land, roll out and stop (5 points) 
• After touchdown, stay below 1.64 ft (0.5 m) AGL and stop on the 
runway. 
• A stop is detected when horizontal ground speed stays below 
1.64 ft/s (0.5 m/s) for at least 1 s. 
• Complete the landing within your class landing limit. 
• A completed landing within the class limit earns 5 points. An 
incomplete landing, or a stop beyond the limit, earns 0 landing 
points. 
▪ Circuit rules (steps 2 to 5) 
• From the end of the initial climb until the final descent, stay 
between 82.0 ft and 114.8 ft (30 ± 5 m) AGL. If the aircraft leaves 
this band, the circuit is not counted. 
• Only the first 360 -degree circuit attempted within the 240 s 
mission time is scored. Additional circuits earn no points. 
• Circuit points are awarded in order. A step scores only when the 
earlier circuit steps are valid. 
• An early first turn, a straight out-and-back path, skipped sectors 
or an incomplete step earn no points for that step. 
o Mission score 
▪ Each mission is worth 25 points:  
• 5 for takeoff (step 1) 
• up to 15 for the circuit (steps 2, 4 and 5, at 5 points each) 
• 5 for landing (step 6) 
▪ The Sea Level Standard mission and the applicable East or West 
competition-site mission have equal weight. 
▪ The flying mission score is the sum of both mission scores, with a 
maximum of 50 points. No environment multiplier is applied.

▪ Each team must submit an Engineering Report  that explains how the 
MathWorks SAE Aero Design Virtual Environment, MATLAB, and Simulink 
were used to address specific project challenges. The report must meet the 
following specifications: 
• Font size: 12 pt proportional font 
• Line spacing: double 
• Layout: Two columns 
• Page size: ANSI A (8.5 × 11 in) 
• Margins: Left: 1.0 in (25.4 mm); Right: 0.5 in (12.7 mm); Top: 0.5 in 
(12.7 mm); Bottom: 0.5 in (12.7 mm) 
• Page limit: 4 pages, including figures,  and tables. Content beyond 
page 4 will not be judged. 
▪ Header at the top of page 1 must show: Team name, School/University, 
Class (Regular/Micro/Advanced) and Competition (East/West). 
▪ File format: PDF, named Competition_Class_TeamNumber_TeamName.pdf 
(for example, East_Micro_101_TeamAlpha.pdf). 
▪ Suggested report structure: Teams may address the following points: 
• Problem and approach: the design problem and why the virtual 
environment/MATLAB/Simulink was used. 
• Aircraft model and simulation setup: what was modeled and what 
was added to the template. 
• Analysis and design insights: plots, trade studies or optimization 
results, and the design decisions they led to. 
• Test and verification: how the model was checked, including 
comparison with ground or flight test data where available. 
• Reflection: what worked well, what was difficult, and which 
MATLAB/Simulink features were useful or missing. 
▪ A panel of MathWorks judges will evaluate the Engineering Report and 
submitted model using the scoring rubric below.

Prepare your submission 
 
1. Add the required files to the appropriate folders in the Virtual Environment project: 
a. Add both selected simulated flight-data files to Submission/Flight Data. 
b. Add the Engineering Report PDF to Submission/Engineering Report. 
2. From the project shortcuts, select Prepare your submission. The MATLAB app will open 
and guide you through packaging the simulation files, flight data, and Engineering Report 
required for submission.
